Relational Databases 101

The ACID Properties

The 4 golden rules that guarantee your database transactions are processed reliably, even in the event of errors, power failures, or crashes.

Atomicity
"All or Nothing"

A transaction must complete 100% successfully. If any single part of the transaction fails, the entire transaction is cancelled (rolled back) as if it never happened.

Bank Transfer Example

You send $100 to a friend. The database deducts $100 from you, but then crashes before adding it to your friend. Atomicity ensures the $100 is returned to you. No money is lost in the void.

Consistency
"Rule-Abiding"

The database must always obey its own rules, constraints, and triggers. A transaction can only bring the database from one valid state to another valid state.

Bank Transfer Example

Your account rule says "Balance cannot go below $0". If you have $50 and try to send $100, Consistency immediately blocks the transaction because it breaks the valid balance rule.

Isolation
"No Interference"

When hundreds of users make requests at the exact same time, transactions execute independently. One transaction cannot read data from another "half-finished" transaction.

Bank Transfer Example

You have $100. You try to buy a $100 shoe on your phone AND a $100 watch on your laptop at the exact same millisecond. Isolation forces the database to process them sequentially in an invisible queue. One succeeds, the other fails. No double-spending!

Durability
"Permanent & Safe"

Once a transaction is successfully completed (committed), the changes are saved to the physical hard drive permanently. They will survive server crashes or power outages.

Bank Transfer Example

You see the green "Transfer Successful" screen. One second later, the bank's data center loses power. Because of Durability, when the servers boot back up, your friend still has the $100.
